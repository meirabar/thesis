# 12. R² vs rho² discrepancy in Table 5
   Status: EXPLAINED (see below)
   Description: User noticed that for 20-DDG Ridge model, med rho ≈ 0.6
   but CV R² ≈ 0.1, whereas rho² ≈ 0.36. Three factors explain this:
   (a) Per-protein median rho vs pooled CV R²: different aggregation.
       Per-protein rho evaluates ranking within each protein independently;
       pooled CV R² measures variance explained across ALL test residues.
       Between-protein heterogeneity in the DDG→RMSF relationship hurts
       pooled R² but does not affect per-protein rho.
   (b) Cross-validation: protein-level CV holds out entire proteins.
       With 20 features, generalization error is significant.
       Compare OLS std(DDG) (1 feature): R²=0.194 vs Ridge 20-DDG: R²=0.105
       — same per-protein rho (~0.59) but very different R² due to
       model complexity under CV.
   (c) Spearman rho ≠ sqrt(R²): rho is rank correlation (monotonic),
       R² is linear variance explained. For nonlinear relationships,
       rho > sqrt(R²).
   Note: the "CV rho" column in ANALYSIS_STATUS.txt (0.419 for 20-DDG)
   is much closer to sqrt(R²)=0.324, confirming the main gap is
   per-protein vs pooled, not rho-vs-R² discrepancy.
